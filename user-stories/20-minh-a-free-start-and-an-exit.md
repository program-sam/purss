# 20 — Minh: the ordinary internet on a student budget

## Fictional first-person scenario

I use my phone for everything: YouTube, games, TikTok, and links people send in
chats. News usually turns up there rather than through a newspaper I follow.
I like the For You feed; it's how I found an animator who also posts on Instagram
and someone making very cheap meals. But favorite tiny creators get lost among
the discoveries. A friend studying abroad posts mostly to Instagram Stories;
remembering to look before they disappear is another thing altogether.

I'm studying design, so studio blogs, lectures, and scholarship notices are mixed
in too. An evening on my Chromebook might drift between assignment ideas, a funny
video, and an article someone shared. I enjoy stumbling across unfamiliar things,
but I also want to find favorites again. Borrowing a university computer
shouldn't mean that all my remembered links are stranded on the phone.

I'm not going to set up a server now, and I'm wary of free trials that need card
details. I've lost course material when a shared space disappeared; I don't want
another small service to become the only place my choices exist. If a free start
has limits, explain them. My university email will disappear after graduation;
I need my account and links to survive that. If I can afford to pay later, great.
If I leave, I'd like something useful to come with me.

## What matters to me

- Keep up with familiar people and publications without technical setup.
- Mix entertainment, friendship, study, and practical opportunities naturally.
- Begin affordably and retain control if devices, money, or services change.
