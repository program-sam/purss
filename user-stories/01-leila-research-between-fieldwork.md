# 01 — Leila: research is only one part of my day

## Fictional first-person scenario

The first thing I open on the train might be a sediment paper, a climbing video,
or a friend's account of moving to another country. I'm finishing a doctorate,
but I don't want every spare moment to become research. I follow journals and lab
blogs alongside a local newspaper, a tiny ceramics channel on YouTube, and two
writers whose essays have nothing to do with my work.

At university I read on a laptop; at home I sometimes put a video on the television
and look up the creator on my phone. I like finding out what people I chose to
follow have made, rather than being pushed toward whatever is popular that day.
A useful idea can cross boundaries: a photographer's explanation of landscapes
might help me communicate fieldwork, but most things are simply for enjoyment.

Before a field trip I need a few specific papers. On a free evening I'd rather
catch up with the people I enjoy. I don't want to reconstruct separate sets of
bookmarks each time my mood changes, or have the weekend reading buried under
journal titles. Last trip, a paper I thought I'd saved needed a university login
I couldn't reach from the field station. I don't want that surprise again.

## What matters to me

- Move between focused work and unstructured enjoyment without losing either.
- Recognize people and publications across articles, videos, and personal updates.
- Return to important references while letting most browsing remain casual.
