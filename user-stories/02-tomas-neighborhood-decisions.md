# 02 — Tomas: an ordinary evening, with a neighborhood issue in the background

## Fictional first-person scenario

After a bus shift I often spend half an hour on my phone. I check the news, see
whether my football club has posted anything, watch a gaming video, and sometimes
see what friends have sent in our WhatsApp group. There are a few small creators
I really like, including a railway modeller who posts progress pictures on Reddit.
I tend to forget to visit them unless a platform happens to remind me.

Lately there's also a road redesign near my flat. I follow the council notices,
a residents' blog, and the local paper because I'd like to have a say. That's
important, but it isn't what I want every visit to the internet to be about. Some
evenings I just want the railway models and the football, not another argument.

Most browsing is on the sofa; longer reading happens on an old laptop. Things
worth remembering range from a recipe for dinner to a public document I'll need
next month. I don't want twenty browser tabs to become the only memory of what
caught my attention. Recommendations don't particularly bother me. I'd only try
another way if it were at least as easy as opening the apps I already use.

## What matters to me

- Catch up with chosen sources without visiting each app separately.
- Give one ongoing local issue some attention without letting it dominate everything.
- Keep useful discoveries easy to find without filing every ordinary click.
