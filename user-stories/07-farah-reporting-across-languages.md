# 07 — Farah: following the world without always being on assignment

## Fictional first-person scenario

As a freelance reporter in a port city, I follow municipal releases, labor
publications, neighborhood blogs, and public social accounts in three languages.
Away from assignments I watch comedy, read film criticism, and follow a baker
whose pop-up dates appear only in Instagram posts. I keep seeing them too late.
Friends send me recommendations in a group chat, where the address of a good
pastry stall is soon buried under jokes.

A phone is where I notice things on the move; my laptop is where I work at home
or in the library. Sometimes work and pleasure overlap: a baker's post leads to
a local business story, or an interview contains a film recommendation. I want
to follow those connections without losing where they began. On other days I
want to stop working and see something that doesn't involve a deadline.

For a real assignment, dates, originals, and later corrections matter. Casual
watching needs none of that ceremony. Confidential interviews stay elsewhere,
and following a public account should not reveal my interests to everyone else.
I need room for both modes, not a choice between a professional monitoring tool
and several unrelated entertainment apps.

## What matters to me

- Find the original wording again, whether it's an interview or a pop-up announcement.
- Switch deliberately between work attention and off-duty browsing.
- Keep a trustworthy source record when needed, without exposing private work.
