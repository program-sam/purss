# 19 — Eli: ordinary browsing on a little machine at home

## Fictional first-person scenario

I follow webcomics, printmaking videos, a local newspaper, friends' blogs, and
someone who makes elaborate game retrospectives. I also keep an eye on a few
independent fan projects that update rarely. My interests aren't especially
technical; the unusual part is that I run some of my online tools on a small
home server because I like having control over my own material.

I browse on a laptop and an Android phone. Sometimes I'm catching up on people,
sometimes looking for a comic I remember, and sometimes just trying to relax
with a video. I don't want my interests to require separate routines for
news, friends, and entertainment, or every update to become an item I must manage.

The server is a little box on a shelf, sharing space with photos and backups.
Recently it stopped accepting family photos and I spent an evening finding out
what had filled it. I want to recognize the material I can safely remove without
gambling with something I deliberately kept or an artist's work that's vanished
from the original site. A future hardware change shouldn't mean rebuilding my
whole everyday internet from memory.

## What matters to me

- Enjoy the same mixed browsing as anyone else, with less dependence on platforms.
- Understand storage before it disrupts unrelated parts of home life.
- Keep deliberate choices portable without becoming a full-time administrator.
