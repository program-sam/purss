# 03 — Nia: my city, my interests, and the practicalities of getting there

## Fictional first-person scenario

My phone is where I follow film critics, a small animation channel, local gig
announcements, and friends' blogs. I also follow an accessibility group and
updates from some venues I visit. I use a wheelchair; knowing about a concert
and finding out whether I can actually get into it are connected parts of the
same evening, not two completely separate interests.

I work across several community venues and often browse from home before going
out. A band's interview might make me look at a venue, and then at a description
of its entrance. I'd like to come back to that little chain later without finding
three social posts and a video all over again. At other times I'm perfectly
happy watching animations and don't need travel planning involved at all.

Some details are time-sensitive. The transport authority's official journey
planner is still where I confirm a trip, especially lift availability. An old
saved page isn't an all-clear. I want practical material to be findable when
needed. If my partner asks which entrance we checked, I want to find that account
quickly rather than start the whole search again at the venue.

## What matters to me

- Let entertainment and practical information connect when they're relevant.
- Choose whether I'm casually browsing or preparing for a particular outing.
- Know who reported an accessible entrance and when, then check before setting off.
