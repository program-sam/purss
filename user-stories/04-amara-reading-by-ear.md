# 04 — Amara: the same everyday internet, mostly by ear

## Fictional first-person scenario

I'm a translator, and I'm blind. During an ordinary day I might listen to a
comedy podcast, read local news, follow an essayist, check a friend's blog, or
play a YouTube interview while making lunch. My paid work involves literature,
but my internet isn't just literary research. I'm also learning to look after
houseplants and follow a wonderfully opinionated small audio-equipment reviewer.

I use screen readers on a laptop and phone. The tiring part is remembering which
site contains which person, then getting through a different maze of controls.
I'd like to keep up with the voices I chose without having to navigate each
platform's recommendations first. I don't want audio starting unexpectedly over
my screen reader just because I passed a video.

Sometimes I want to settle into a long essay; sometimes I only have time to hear
what a friend has been doing. A useful piece about a plant problem and an interview
I want for work both deserve to be findable later, but they aren't the same kind
of commitment. Some sites give me only a teaser; I'd like to know that before
settling in to listen, and reach the original without losing my place.

## What matters to me

- Browse ordinary mixed interests with predictable, properly named controls.
- Choose between reading and listening, rather than having media interrupt me.
- Pick up interrupted pieces without needing to organize my entire life first.
