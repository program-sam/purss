# 09 — Jules: the people I actually came online to see

## Fictional first-person scenario

I don't think of myself as a power user. I watch YouTube on the sofa, follow a
friend's renovation pictures on Instagram, and check a handful of creators making
music, game videos, and oddly specific food experiments. News mostly reaches me
through links other people send. A few creators are so small that I remember their
faces more readily than their channel names.

Music is my deeper interest: little labels on Bandcamp, community radio, musicians' own pages,
and two local venues. But I don't want a music application to become another
separate destination. In the same evening I might watch a game review, read a
long article, and notice a gig I'd like to attend. I like recommendations when
they find me a new band. But I missed a favorite label's release while listening
to all those discoveries, and only heard about it after the small vinyl run sold out.
If a platform won't show their updates elsewhere, tell me; silence shouldn't make
me assume an artist has stopped making things.

I use a phone while walking and a laptop at home. Most things need nothing beyond
a pleasant few minutes. Occasionally I want to come back later, buy an album,
or send something to a friend. Keeping up should still work after a busy fortnight
without my having to clear a wall of old updates first.

## What matters to me

- Get to familiar creators and chosen publications with little fuss.
- Mix ordinary entertainment, personal updates, and a deeper interest naturally.
- Leave when I've had enough and return without feeling behind.
