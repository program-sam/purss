# Independent review guide

## Purpose

Assess whether the proposed product is meaningfully useful to each fictional
person. The goal is not twenty positive verdicts. These narratives are exploratory
prompts, not evidence that demand exists. No assessment has been performed yet.
Editorial review of the narratives is separate from assessing product fit: approval
of a story does not establish that the planned product can serve it.

## Review method

1. Read the assigned narrative before reading the product plan. Extract the
   person's everyday mix of interests, relationships, responsibilities, context,
   constraints, and non-negotiable outcomes. Do not reduce them to their occupation
   or specialist interest, or treat leisure as a task that needs completion.
2. Read the plan separately and distinguish implemented behavior (none yet),
   planned first slice, later milestones, hypotheses, and explicit non-goals.
3. Assess source access independently. Native feeds, a usable public URL, private
   portals, email, account-only content, and emergency alerts are not interchangeable.
   Mark unverified platform access unknown rather than assuming a connector exists.
4. Identify a plausible end-to-end day, or explain why none is plausible. Examine
   the first failure point, setup/maintenance effort, privacy/accessibility/storage
   implications, and any simpler alternative. State disconfirming evidence explicitly.
   Consider switching between moods and interests, incidental discoveries, and
   returning after time away. A specialist tool that solves only the niche task
   does not by itself establish fit for the whole person. Conversely, using PURSS
   need not mean one mixed stream, or replacing chats, private portals, and specialist
   tools. Do not require every story to use the same sources or behaviors.
   An enjoyable, engaging visit is a valid outcome. Do not assume people must reject
   recommendations or dislike badges, new-content cues, or notifications to benefit
   from personal curation; assess their preferences and control instead.
5. Give a candid verdict: strong proposed fit, partial fit, poor fit, or insufficient
   evidence. Out of scope/better served elsewhere and an unrealistic premise are
   valid findings too. Explain the decisive need; do not use a feature count.

## Suggested assessment record

- Story ID and reviewer, with exact plan snapshot reviewed.
- User job and indispensable outcomes, in the person's words where useful.
- Source/access assumptions, supported by evidence or explicitly unknown.
- First-slice fit, later-plan fit, and what is not promised at all.
- A concrete failure or friction point and its significance to this person.
- Whether an alternative would be simpler or safer.
- Verdict, uncertainty, and questions for a real interview or usability session.

Do not rewrite the story, infer a medical or safety guarantee, assume paid/private
content can be captured, invent product functionality, or read other reviewers'
conclusions before giving an independent assessment. Adding a source to a feed
does not automatically provide notifications, offline playback, or full archives.
Record future assessments separately so the original briefs remain neutral.
