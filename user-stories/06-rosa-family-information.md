# 06 — Rosa: still a person online, not only the family organizer

## Fictional first-person scenario

A lot of my phone time is practical: school emails, library events, swimming-club
notices. But I also follow a cooking channel, a few funny video creators, local
news, and a friend who writes thoughtful posts about the books she reads. Sometimes
I realize I've spent my whole evening checking other people's schedules and never
reached anything I wanted for myself.

I browse in short bursts, often with one hand free, and use a tablet on Sunday.
An appealing recipe, a video I want to show my partner, a library event, and an
article I want to finish can all turn up within ten minutes. I don't want four
separate places to remember them. My partner and I often remember different halves
of the same recipe video; being able to send the actual source would save an argument.

Some information is private, such as the school portal. I won't give an unfamiliar
service my whole email account just to follow one newsletter. If those sources
must stay separate, I'd rather understand that than assume every part of the day
has been gathered. What I want most is less checking and a little more room for
ordinary curiosity, not another system I must administer for everyone.

## What matters to me

- Move easily between practical updates and things that are simply enjoyable.
- Remember a few worthwhile discoveries without sorting every message.
- Keep private family material private and understand what's missing.
