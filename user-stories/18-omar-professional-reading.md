# 18 — Omar: life online around rotating shifts

## Fictional first-person scenario

My working hours move around, so I don't have a fixed morning reading habit.
I use my phone for football, cooking videos, friends' posts, and a couple of
creators who explain obscure bits of film history. I also follow a professional
association, some journal sections, and a podcast about communication because I'm
a nurse and want to keep learning.

After a night shift, I often watch on the television while eating. A video I
noticed on my phone can be surprisingly hard to find there. On a day off I may
want to concentrate on a study. I'd like both kinds of attention to be possible
without maintaining entirely different sets of bookmarks and subscriptions.
Something worth revisiting shouldn't disappear just because the original moment
for reading it passed.

Work material has special boundaries. A commentary isn't a guideline, and an
abstract isn't a complete paper. Patient information and clinical decisions stay
in my employer's approved systems. The film-history videos are something my brother
and I talk about; I'd like a familiar place for chosen sources that doesn't confuse
the two and remembers where I stopped.

## What matters to me

- Follow ordinary interests and professional reading on an irregular schedule.
- Choose depth when I have energy and light browsing when I don't.
- Keep confidential work out and preserve source distinctions when they matter.
